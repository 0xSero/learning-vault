# Methodism

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> Methodism is a family of Protestant churches, Wesleyan in origin, that holds salvation is available to all people (Arminian rather than Calvinist), that holiness is possible in this life (not just after death), and that faith must issue in works of mercy. Structurally it is **connexional**: every local church is linked in a global network, not autonomous. One line: a movement that started as a revival inside the Church of England and became a worldwide communion of ~80 million.

## Origins (18th-century Britain)

- Began as a revival movement *inside* the Church of England. John Wesley (1703–1791), his brother Charles, and a small group called the "Holy Club" were mocked as **"Methodists"** for their methodical devotion — the name stuck.
- The turning point: Wesley's conversion at **Aldersgate** (May 24, 1738), shaped by Moravian piety — his feeling that his heart was "strangely warmed" with trust in Christ for forgiveness.
- It only became a separate church when the American Revolution cut the American movement off from Anglican bishops: Wesley ordained ministers for America in 1784; Britain separated formally in 1795.

## Core theology (what makes it "Methodist")

- **Arminian soteriology.** Prevenient grace goes to everyone, so the offer of salvation is genuinely universal; people can respond freely, and (the "watchman" position) can fall away. No double predestination.
- **Entire sanctification** — the signature teaching. After justification, there is a second work of grace in which the heart is renewed and the person made "perfect in love" — not sinless, but freed from deliberate sin. The 1744 General Conference's title, *"Scriptural Holiness Preached in All Its Consequences,"* is the movement's motto.
- **Faith and works.** Justification is by faith alone, but "the faith that justifies is never alone" — works are the fruit, not the price.
- **Means of grace.** How God actually conveys grace: the two sacraments (baptism, the Lord's Supper — frequently received, open communion) plus the "ordinances": preaching, prayer, Scripture study, and small groups (Wesley's **class meetings**, the ancestor of today's small groups).
- **Sufficiency of Scripture** as the final authority.

## Structure (connexionalism)

Local churches → annual conferences → bishops → the **General Conference** (supreme, meets every 4 years). Clergy are *appointed* by the conference to serve a church rather than hired by it — Wesley's line: *"the world is my parish."* The fourfold ministry: bishop, elder, deacon, laity.

## The family tree today

- **United Methodist Church (UMC)** — 1968 merger of the Methodist Church (itself a 1939 merger of the northern and southern Methodist churches, split in 1844 over slavery) and the Evangelical United Brethren. The largest branch. Peaked around 12 million US members in the 1960s; now roughly half that and declining, with growth concentrated in Africa and Asia.
- **Methodist Church of Great Britain**, **Methodist Church of Korea** (very large), **Australia**, **Japan**, **India**, and dozens of national churches.
- **World Methodist Council** (since 1924) — the global umbrella: ~80 member churches, ~80 million members worldwide.
- **Free Methodist** (1860) and the **Wesleyan Church** (1934) — holiness-lineage branches, stricter on sanctification.
- **Black and African Methodism** grew out of the movement's early openness to Black membership, including the **CME (Christian Methodist Episcopal)** church.

## What it's known for in practice

- **Hymns.** Charles Wesley wrote 6,000+ hymns ("Holy, Holy, Holy," "Come, Thou Fount," "Count Your Blessings"). Hymnody is a core Methodist identity marker.
- **Social holiness / social gospel.** Works of mercy as non-negotiable. Methodists drove abolition, temperance, labor organizing, and the civil rights movement (MLK was a UMC member), and the denomination built a huge hospital and university network (Duke, Emory, Vanderbilt, SMU, and the "Methodist Hospital" tradition).
- **Itinerant preaching.** Circuits of one pastor serving several towns; revivalism as a permanent feature.

## Current tension

The UMC's internal conflict over LGBTQ ordination (its Book of Discipline bars it; a 2021 disaffiliation plan let congregations leave to form the **Global Methodist Church** in 2023) is actively reshaping the denomination, and the church is in the middle of revising its stance — that debate is what most contemporary "Methodism news" is about.
