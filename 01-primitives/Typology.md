# Typology

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> Typology is the reading method where an earlier real event (the **type**) prefigures a later one (the **antitype**): the mountain with the son (Gen 22) points to the cross; the manna points to the bread of life; the bronze serpent points to the lifted-up Son of man. The early church ran the whole OT this way, and it is the method that makes [[The Sacrifice Thread]] work. The rule that keeps it honest: a type must be historically real, and the fulfillment must *exceed* the type.

## The mechanism

A type is not an allegory. Allegory says "this story *means* that." Typology says "this thing *happened*, and history then did a bigger version of it." The two events are both real; the second one reveals what the first one was secretly doing.

The technical term in Hebrews 11:19 is the giveaway: Abraham offered Isaac, "accounting that God was able to raise him from the dead; from whence also he received him **in a figure**" (Greek *en typos* — "as in a figure/pattern"). The verse says the mountain was a *pattern* of the resurrection. That is typology in one phrase.

## The canonical examples

| Type (OT) | Antitype (NT) | Text of the link |
|---|---|---|
| The ram provided for Isaac (Gen 22:13) | Christ, the substitute | Heb 11:19; the Fathers' standard reading |
| Manna from heaven (Ex 16) | "The true bread from heaven" — Jesus's flesh | John 6:31–35, 48–51 |
| Bronze serpent lifted up (Num 21:9) | "Even so must the Son of man be lifted up" | John 3:14–15 |
| Passover lamb (Ex 12) | "Behold the Lamb of God" | John 1:29 |
| Temple / tabernacle (Ex 25–40) | "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up" (his body) | John 2:19–21 |
| The suffering servant (Isa 53) | The crucifixion | Isa 53:7–12 → the whole passion narrative |

## The rules that keep it honest

1. **Historical reality:** the type actually happened. "The mountain" is not a symbol the author invented to encode a doctrine — Abraham, Isaac, the knife, and the ram are events.
2. **Exceeding:** the antitype must be greater than the type. The ram was one animal; the cross is the Son. If the "fulfillment" is just the type repeated, it's not typology, it's repetition.
3. **Direction:** the type points forward; the antitype cannot retroactively redefine the type's history. Gen 22's *stated* purpose (a test, "to prove thee," v. 2) stays what it is — typology does not erase the text's own intent, it shows what the text later revealed itself to be.
4. **The reader's limit:** typology is a *reading method*, not a proof. It explains why the thread connects; it does not compel the connection. The strongest typologies (manna → John 6) are the ones where the NT text *itself* makes the link explicitly.

## The early church ran on typology

Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, and the rest read the OT almost entirely as a pattern-book for Christ. The Isaac reading (one son, a mountain, wood carried on the son's shoulders, a single substitute) became one of the most repeated typologies in patristic literature. [[The Sacrifice Thread]] is a modern re-derivation of that same reading, stress-tested against the text's own words.
