# John 8:40

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> "But now ye seek to kill me, a man that hath told you the truth, which I have heard of God: this did not Abraham." One sentence, and Jesus defines himself twice and turns their genealogy against them. He answers "who are you?" the way Moses would — not with a title, but with a source: *a man, speaking what he heard from God* — then holds up Abraham as the mirror in which the killers see they are no longer the father's children.

## Context

John 8:12–59 is one sustained confrontation in the temple, the second half of it after the man born blind (chapter 9 in the narrative flow; 8:12–59 is the dispute). They have just claimed, "We have one Father, even God" (v. 39, the verse before), and he has just told them the Scriptures testify of him (v. 39). Verse 40 is his counter. The charge they bring against him is not sin — in v. 46 he asks "which of you convinceth me of sin?" — it is only that they want him silent.

## The Greek

> νῦν οὖν ζητεῖτε ἀποκτεῖναί με, τὸν ἄνθρωπον τὴν ἀλήθειαν εἰπὸν ἣν ἠκούσα παρὰ θεοῦ; τοῦτο οὐκ ἐποίησεν Ἀβραάμ.

Four elements, each doing work:

### 1. "a man" (τὸν ἄνθρωπον) — no title
He doesn't say "the Son of God" or "the Christ." Just *the man* who spoke the truth. Deliberate understatement — his preferred self-presentation in John strips off honorifics (cf. [[Son of Man]] for the other direction). He defines himself by **office, not title**: the truth-teller.

### 2. "the truth which I heard" (τὴν ἀλήθειαν ἣν ἠκούσα)
The aorist ἠκούσα is past-tense hearing: he *received* the truth by audition. This is the key claim. In Second Temple Judaism, "he heard from God" was the mark of the highest possible prophetic office — the level of Moses, "to whom he spake face to face" (Deut 34:10), the prophet "like unto whom" God would raise up (Deut 18:15, which Acts 3:22 applies to Jesus). Jesus is saying: my message is not my own, not the rabbis' tradition, not an interpretation of the law — it is what I heard directly from God.

Note the article-less παρὰ θεοῦ — "from God": it is the source itself, not "the God you worship" (cf. the next verse, where he contrasts *his* Father with *their* father; v. 41 and v. 44).

### 3. "this did not Abraham" (τοῦτο οὐκ ἐποίησεν Ἀβραάμ)
The turn. Their claim is Abraham; his answer is that the man who speaks the truth he heard from God is now being hunted by the very descendants of Abraham — and Abraham, the man who *believed God* (Gen 15:6), never killed a truth-teller sent by God. Their own tradition condemns what they're doing: the killing of prophets runs "from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zacharias" (Luke 11:51; Matt 23:35).

The pincer that follows: they do "the works of your father" (v. 41) → "your father the devil" (v. 44) → "your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad" (v. 56). The whole passage holds their claimed father against their actual behavior.

### 4. The implicit claim
The verse is also a quiet identity statement: *I am the truth-teller whom God sent, and you are killing him.* It is why the same chapter ends with the unpredicated "Before Abraham was, I am" (v. 58) and them taking up stones. See [[I Am Formula]].

## Across the translations

| Version | Rendering |
|---|---|
| KJV | "a man that hath told you the truth, which I have heard of God: this did not Abraham." |
| ASV | "a man that hath told you the truth, which I heard from God: this did not Abraham." |
| WEB | "a man who has told you the truth, which I heard from God. Abraham didn't do this." |
| NIV (paraphrase of sense) | "a man who has told you the truth I have heard from God. Abraham did not do such a thing." |

All versions preserve the three-part structure (the man / the truth heard / Abraham's contrast). What shifts is the feel: the KJV's archaic "hath… heard" reads like a standing state of office; the WEB's "Abraham didn't do this" flattens the formal weight of τοῦτο οὐκ ἐποίησεν Ἀβραάμ.

The translation loss common to all English versions: "a man" (*ton anthrōpon*) carries no title in the Greek, yet in English it reads as *any* man rather than "the man, full stop."

## The number 23, while we're at it

The verse sits in a chapter whose number carries a trinity of symbols in the biblical frame: **Genesis 23** (the purchase of the tomb — the first land possessed, and it is a grave), **Psalm 23** (the shepherd), **Luke 23** (the passion chapter). A "23" in the biblical frame points to the tomb, the shepherd, and the cross at once. See [[The 23-Second Probe]] for why a *duration* of 23 carries no fixed symbol of its own.
